From resource allocation to energy

A research prototype for inspecting scheduling tradeoffs. The allocation engine is an abstraction, not a full 3GPP implementation. All displayed power is estimated.

Grid and traffic

NR uses 51 PRBs, 30 kHz spacing and 0.5 ms slots; LTE uses 100 PRBs and a 1 ms scheduling interval. Both have 14 average-duration symbol bins per interval. A PRB spans 12 subcarriers. LTE's 1 ms display combines two physical slots. Cyclic-prefix duration differences are averaged. Each cell represents one PRB for one symbol; it is not a single resource element.

Poisson packet arrivals use a fixed seed and six UE IDs. Effective payload bits per cell depend on UE and a fixed 0.8 payload fraction. The fixed PRB signalling mask is illustrative: three PRBs on the first symbol of every interval, plus symbol 7 for LTE. It does not implement CRS, SSB, DM-RS, PDCCH or TDD patterns faithfully.

Controlled comparison

Always-on and micro-sleep use the same frequency-major allocation order. Compact uses earliest-deadline-first and time-major packing, with a bounded hold for flexible traffic; urgent packets bypass the hold. Arrivals and payload capacity are identical across policies. Arrivals are available only at interval boundaries. Pending packets are retained past deadlines and reported as missed, not silently dropped.

Power and sleep

Pactive = Pidle + slope × occupied PRB fraction. Occupancy includes signalling. A whole-symbol gap is required for sleep; idle frequency resources alone do not justify PA shutdown. Gaps need two entry symbols, at least one sleep symbol, and one wake symbol. Sleep is selected only when the estimated saved energy exceeds wake cost. Energy is the sum of P × symbol_duration, with wake energy integrated once per transition. Default values (180 W idle, 120 W load slope, 45 W sleep, 0.15 mJ wake) are scenario assumptions, not Ericsson hardware measurements.

Evaluation boundaries

Throughput includes transmitted portions of unfinished packets; latency uses completed packets. Incomplete packets with deadlines after the horizon are censored; others count as deadline misses. Report backlog and completion alongside latency. Delivery fairness is Jain's index of per-UE delivered/offered fractions. No propagation, HARQ, MIMO, multi-cell interference, mobility, cooling or calibrated energy prediction is implemented. SSE is paced simulation replay; the separate ingestion API accepts externally supplied allocation frames, with estimated power. Telemetry frames use independent per-frame gap accounting and are not a continuous hardware sleep controller.
